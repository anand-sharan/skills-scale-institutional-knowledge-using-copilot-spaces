# OctoAcme Project Management Process Documentation

Welcome to the OctoAcme project management documentation hub. This landing page provides a brief overview of OctoAcme's end-to-end project management processes and links to all detailed process documents.

---

## Overview

OctoAcme's project management process follows a clear lifecycle—**initiation, planning, execution, release, and retrospective improvement**—designed to keep work outcome-driven, predictable, and continuously improving.

**Initiation** validates business need before work begins. Each project is scoped through a lightweight one-pager that captures success metrics, stakeholders, timeline, early risks, and resource needs before receiving approval to proceed.

**Planning** translates approved goals into a prioritized backlog with acceptance criteria, estimates, dependencies, milestones, and a defined Definition of Done. This structure anchors teams to outcomes while making day-to-day execution trackable.

**Execution & Tracking** is organized around a strong delivery rhythm—standups, weekly delivery syncs, and milestone demos—alongside a visible workflow board (Backlog → Ready → In Progress → In Review → QA → Done). Progress is measured through velocity, burndown, and key product signals (usage, error rates, latency), with a defined escalation path for blockers. Disciplined pull request practices (small PRs, issue linkage, CI checks, required approvals) keep code quality high throughout delivery.

**Risk & Communication** are treated as core operating mechanisms. OctoAcme maintains a formal risk register (impact, likelihood, owner, mitigation, status), conducts weekly risk reviews, and uses recurring status updates and single sources of truth to keep teams and stakeholders aligned. Incident communication follows a structured triage-and-update flow, including post-incident retrospectives.

**Release & Deployment** includes release readiness checks, layered testing (unit, integration, end-to-end smoke tests), CI linting and security scanning, manual QA as needed, rollback planning, and post-release verification to ensure stable, safe releases.

**Retrospective & Continuous Improvement** closes each cycle by turning lessons learned into tracked action items. Teams review what went well, what didn't, and what to change—keeping the process itself improving over time.

**Roles & Personas** are cross-functional but clearly separated: *Project Managers* coordinate execution, risks, timelines, and stakeholder reporting; *Product Managers* define outcomes and prioritize the backlog; *Developers* implement, test, and surface technical risks; *QA* verifies quality against acceptance criteria; and *Stakeholders* support alignment and approvals at key milestones.
